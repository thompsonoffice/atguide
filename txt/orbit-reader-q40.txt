Detailed Description:
The Orbit Reader Q40 combines a 40-cell braille display with a full typewriter keyboard in one case. It is about 11 inches wide, 7 inches deep and a little over an inch high, and weighs about two and a half pounds. Orbit describes it as dust- and fluid-resistant.
The keyboard is a full-size, laptop-style layout of 78 sculpted keys. It has a full row of twelve function keys, arrow keys in an inverted T, and Windows and Mac keys, but no separate numeric keypad.
The display has 40 eight-dot cells using Orbit's TrueBraille mechanism, with a cursor routing button for each cell. A rocker key at each end of the display pans the text, and configurable thumb keys help with navigation.
For connections it has a USB-C port for charging and computers, a USB-A host port for flash drives, and a full-size SD card slot.
It gives audio and haptic feedback, and has a fast-charging, long-life battery.

History:
Orbit Research announced the Q40 and 20-cell Q20 on March 19, 2024 (press release datelined Anaheim), pairing the Orbit Reader with a laptop-style keyboard; shipping was planned for June 2024. It works standalone as a book reader and note-taker, or as a display connected to up to six devices over USB and Bluetooth with JAWS, NVDA, VoiceOver, TalkBack and others. It translates braille in more than 70 languages and works with Orbit Teacher and Orbit Chat.
Predecessors: Orbit Reader 40 (Orbit Research, Announced March 11, 2020 (CSUN))
