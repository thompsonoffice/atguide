Detailed Description:
Dolphin's 1998 Series 2 user guide describes the Apollo 2 as a mains-only desktop synthesizer with a built-in loudspeaker, measuring 11.5 cm long, 13 cm wide and 7 cm high. Its siblings were the PC 2 card (the same synthesizer on an internal PC card) and the Juno, a pocket-sized battery unit.
With the speaker facing you, there are three connectors. On the back is a 9-way D-type serial socket for the computer; it is wired in a non-standard way, so only Dolphin's cable (9-way female at both ends, reversible) should be used, and the guide warns that a standard cable may damage the synthesizer's port. Also at the back is the socket for the mains adapter.
On the front is a 3.5 mm stereo socket for headphones or an extension speaker; the guide says a mono plug will not work correctly. The adapter must supply 13 V at 300 mA through a 2.1 mm barrel, negative tip.
There are only two controls. The on/off switch is at the back on the left-hand side (left is on, right is off), and the volume knob is on top, to the left, just below the speaker grille. At switch-on the unit tests itself: one short buzz means it passed, three longer hoots mean it failed.
Inside, the Apollo 2 holds up to four languages in replaceable ROMs (one 64K ROM as standard) and was one of the few synthesizers with a British English voice. The original Apollo that preceded the Series 2 is not described in any source found; this description covers the Apollo 2.

History:
Dolphin built the Apollo hardware speech synthesizer as the voice for its Hal for DOS screen reader. Dolphin says the combination was available in more than 30 languages.  Dolphin's 1998 Series 2 user guide says its synthesizers were developed alongside the Hal screen reader. The family offered more than forty languages, with up to seven in one synthesizer. The Apollo 2 was a mains-powered desktop unit with a built-in loudspeaker that connected to a PC's serial port with a non-standard Dolphin cable, and held up to four languages in replaceable ROMs. It was one of the few synthesizers with a British English voice. Its siblings were the PC 2 internal card and the Juno, a pocket-sized battery unit with about 10 hours of battery life. In 2026 the Dolphin Apollo II Reborn project on GitHub decompiled the Apollo 2 firmware into C.
