Detailed Description:
The Index Computer Braille Printer was the first embosser made by what became Index Braille. Braillecast says a small batch was built in Björn Löfstedt's garage in Sweden by 1984.
These machines became known as 'Index 3.7' embossers, after their firmware version.
They came out of a 1979 university project to build a braille typewriter that could also make copies. Polar Print Production, the company Löfstedt and Torvald Lundqvist formed in 1982, built them.
Index Braille became a brand in its own right in the late 1980s with the Index Blue Bar, a tractor-feed embosser, followed in 1992 by the cut-sheet Everest. SAST describes the first-generation (V1) Basic as having a steel case with a blue and red bar, but no source ties that description to the Index 3.7 itself.
No photograph, museum record, manual or contemporary review of the Index 3.7 turned up in this research. Its size, weight, speed, paper handling and interfaces are therefore not included.

History:
Index began in 1979 as a university project to build a braille typewriter with a copy function. Björn Löfstedt was inspired by his mother, who went blind at 32. With support from a Swedish disability organization, he and Torvald Lundqvist founded Polar Print Production (later Index Braille AB) in 1982. A small batch of Index Computer Braille Printers was built in Löfstedt's garage in 1984; they became known as the 'Index 3.7' after the firmware version. Index moved into its current premises in 1985, paid for by distributing assistive technology around Sweden.
Successors: Index Blue Bar (Index Braille, 1988)
