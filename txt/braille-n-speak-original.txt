Braille 'n Speak (original)
Company: Blazie Engineering
Category: Notetakers
Released: 1987 to 1990s

Overview:
A pocket notetaker with a six-key Perkins-style braille keyboard and speech output, introduced at the 1987 NFB convention for $895.

Specs:
Released: 1987 to 1990s
Processor: Not documented
Memory (RAM): 200K nonvolatile memory (1988 review)
Storage: Not documented
Operating system: Not documented
Default speech synthesizer: Built-in synthesizer (name not documented)
Battery: Not documented
Display or braille cells: Not documented
Price at launch: $895

Physical Description:
A 1988 review gives about 8 x 4 x 1 inches and under 1 lb. AFB describes the device as 2 lb. Braille keys plus space bar.

Features:
Braille input, speech output
Talking calculator, clock and calendar
File storage in nonvolatile memory

History:
Deane Blazie's Blazie Engineering designed it as an affordable portable for braille readers. It led to the Braille 'n Speak Classic, the Braille 'n Speak 2000, the Type 'n Speak and the Braille Lite. AFB reported about 70,000 Blazie notetakers in use. The line passed to Freedom Scientific in 2000.
Successors: Braille 'n Speak Classic (Blazie Engineering, Name later given to the original line); Braille 'n Speak 2000 (Blazie Engineering, 1997); Braille Lite 18 (Blazie Engineering, Summer 1993)
