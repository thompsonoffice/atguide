History:
APH and Orbit Research announced it on August 2, 2016 as the world's first affordable refreshable tactile graphics display, built on Orbit's Tactuator technology. Each of its 2,400 pins, in a 60 by 40 array, can rise to different heights to represent topographic maps and shades of gray or color, and can blink individually. It connects by USB-C to computers, tablets, phones or the Orion TI-84 Plus talking graphing calculator, and has an HDMI port that makes it appear as a monitor. It also has a cursor pad, an SD card slot for standalone use, and a touch interface where tracing a shape with a fingertip raises the pins along the path.
