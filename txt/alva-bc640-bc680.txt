Detailed Description:
The ALVA BC640 is a compact 40-cell braille display. The cells sit at the back of its flat top, with a cursor-routing key in front of each cell. The 80-cell BC680 shares the same design.
On each side of the braille line are two eTouch keys for panning and screen-reader functions. The top keys are marked with two vertical lines and the bottom keys with one horizontal line.
In the middle, in front of the routing keys, is the Smartpad of nine keys: two rectangular keys on the left, a five-way navigation key (Left, Up, Down, Right and a dot-marked Middle), and two rectangular keys on the right. Along the front edge are five thumb keys: four large ones for left, right, up and down, and a smaller middle one that returns to the cursor.
The power switch is a rectangular push button on the right side, and it beeps when switched on. The left side holds, from front to back, the power socket, a mini-USB port and a feature connector. A connector at the back attaches the Braille Audio Feature Pack.
The Feature Pack clips on in front and adds an eight-key braille keyboard with a space bar, Ctrl, Windows, Enter and Alt keys, stereo speakers, headphone and microphone jacks, and a volume and mute control. The display connects by USB or Bluetooth (a Class 1 USB dongle was included) and runs on an internal battery when not powered over USB.

History:
Optelec's user manual, copyright 2007, introduces the ALVA Braille Controller 6 series as the first series of wireless braille controllers to combine classic braille display access with advanced functions. The series offered Bluetooth and USB in an ultra-compact design for computers, PDAs and smartphones. There were two models: the 40-cell BC640, which could take an optional Braille Audio Feature Pack with a braille keyboard and stereo speakers, and the 80-cell BC680, with a USB keyboard connection. Battery life was about 15 hours for the BC640, 40 hours with the Feature Pack, and 10 hours for the BC680.
