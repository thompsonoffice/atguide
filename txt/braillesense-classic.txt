BrailleSense (Classic)
Company: HIMS / Selvas BLV
Category: BrailleSense
Released: 2004

Overview:
The first BrailleSense (the renamed Braille Hansone), a 32-cell notetaker running Windows CE.NET.

Specs:
Released: 2004
Processor: Intel PXA255
Memory (RAM): 64 MB
Storage: 64 MB flash
Operating system: Windows CE.NET
Default speech synthesizer: An onboard speech engine. ACB's review notes it was the only engine available at the time; the engine is not named.
Battery: Detachable lithium-ion
Display or braille cells: 32 cells with 32 routing buttons; LCD
Speech synthesizers available: Not documented
Price at launch: $4,950 (Braille Hansone, early 2005)
Used price today (eBay): Not documented

Physical Description:
9 x 5 x 1.5 in, 2.7 lb. Eight-key braille keyboard (dot 7 is Backspace, dot 8 is Enter) with F1 to F4. The front edge has microphone and headphone jacks and Previous, Record, Stop, Play and Next buttons.

Detailed Description:
The first BrailleSense was sold at first as the Braille Hansone, a Korean name meaning 'to see the world through one fingertip'. HIMS renamed it BrailleSense for English speakers. AccessWorld measured it at 9 × 5 × 1.5 inches and 2.7 pounds.
On top is an eight-key braille keyboard: dot 7 is Backspace and dot 8 is Enter. Four oval function keys sit beside the space bar, F1 and F2 on the left and F3 and F4 on the right. F1 opens the program menu, F2 the menu of the current program, F3 is Tab and F4 is Escape.
In the middle, above the keyboard, is a small LCD screen so sighted people can follow along, with a stereo speaker at each edge above Backspace and Enter.
Below the keyboard is a 32-cell braille display with 32 routing buttons. At the right end of the routing row is an Advance key and at the left end a Scroll Back key.
The front panel, from left to right, has a microphone jack, a stereo headphone jack and five audio buttons: Previous, Record, Stop, Play/Pause and Next. The right side has the power switch, AC socket and detachable lithium-ion battery; the left side has two CompactFlash slots. The back has infrared, two USB, LAN, parallel, video-out and serial ports.

Features:
32-cell display with 32 routing buttons
LCD screen
Two stereo speakers
Voice recorder and DAISY player controls on the front edge
Up to seven open applications
Detachable lithium-ion battery
HBL braille document format; no spell checker at review time

History:
GW Micro distributed it in the US. AccessWorld (January 2005) found it lighter than the BrailleNote and PAC Mate.
Predecessors: Braille Hansone (HIMS / Selvas BLV, 2002)
Successors: BrailleSense Plus (HIMS / Selvas BLV, 2008); BrailleSense LX (HIMS / Selvas BLV, 2008)

Ports:
2 CompactFlash slots, infrared, 2 USB, LAN, parallel, VGA video out, serial, microphone and headphone jacks

Windows CE.NET

Operating system history: HIMS BrailleSense software
