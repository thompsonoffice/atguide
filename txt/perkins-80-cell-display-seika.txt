Detailed Description:
The Perkins 80-cell display is the Seika 80, made by Nippon Telesoft of Tokyo. It has 80 eight-dot braille cells in a single long, slim case.
Nippon Telesoft lists it at 58 × 9 × 2.5 cm (23 × 3.5 × 0.8 in) and 1,000 g; its English page also gives 3.3 lb.
There is a touch cursor (routing) key above each of the 80 cells, plus 8 function keys, including pan-left and pan-right keys and thumb keys.
It connects to the computer with one USB cable and draws its power over USB, so it has no battery or power adapter. It came with a leather carrying case and strap.
Nippon Telesoft lists support for NVDA, JAWS, SuperNova, Window-Eyes and COBRA, and supplies driver and screen-reader patch downloads.

History:
In its report on the summer 2010 blindness conventions, AccessWorld singled out this display as one of two products that drew attention for their low cost and high value. Perkins Products was selling the 80-cell refreshable braille display for $3,900, then a remarkably low price for 80 cells. It was made by Seika, connected by USB, and was supported by all the major Windows screen readers and by Apple's VoiceOver.
