Detailed Description:
The Compact+ is a handheld video magnifier about 5 inches long, 3 inches wide and 1.25 inches thick, and it weighs 10.6 ounces. Most of the top is a 4-inch LCD screen.
Next to the screen is a single round control. Turning a lever on it changes the magnification between three levels, 5x, 7.5x and 10x, and the same control switches the viewing modes. A Snapshot feature freezes the picture so you can hold it closer or show it to someone, and you can magnify the frozen image too.
Underneath, the camera is attached to the battery compartment, which slides along a track. Move it directly under the screen for reading, or slide it to the side to leave room for a pen when writing.
A collapsible handgrip also folds out from the underside and snaps into place, so you can hold the unit like a traditional magnifying glass.
It runs on a pack of two rechargeable AA batteries, which charge in the unit in about three hours and last about as long. Ordinary store-bought AA batteries also work. The box included a carrying case, a neck strap and a power supply. The manual is a spiral-bound, postcard-size booklet printed in 14-point type.

History:
AccessWorld described the Compact+ in September 2007 as new to the market and a complete redesign of Optelec's earlier handheld Compact. It cost $795, the same as its rival, the Enhanced Vision Nemo. The reviewer found it smaller, lighter and easier to hold, with a single one-finger control, but with a dimmer, more glare-prone screen. Optelec said it was the only product in its class to run on rechargeable AA batteries.
