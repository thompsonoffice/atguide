Detailed Description:
The Braille Lite M20 and M40 are flat, rectangular notetakers. The M20 is the shorter model with a 20-cell display, and the M40 is wider to hold a 40-cell display. Exact dimensions and weight are not documented in the sources used.
The top surface has an eight-dot Perkins-style braille keyboard: six main dot keys plus dots 7 and 8, with a space bar between the hands. Almost every command is a 'chord', meaning the space bar pressed together with dot keys.
Below the keyboard is the refreshable braille display. Above each braille cell is a small cursor routing button that puts the cursor at that cell when pressed. The M40 also has raised locator dots above every fifth cell to help you find your place.
Advance bars let you move through a file one display-length at a time: press the right end to go forward and the left end to go back. The M20 has one advance bar; the M40 has two, one at each end, so you never have to lift your reading hands far. A Reverse Advance setting swaps the bar directions.
The Millennium models added programmable Whiz Wheels, a CompactFlash card slot and a built-in modem. An Italian product database notes the 56K modem for e-mail and the side-mounted wheels for moving through documents. Speech comes from an internal synthesizer through a built-in speaker or headphones.

History:
The Braille Lite M Series ('Millennium') was the last generation of Braille Lite notetakers. Freedom Scientific's manual describes a redesign with about 2 MB RAM, 12 MB flash, a CompactFlash port, a modem and programmable Whiz Wheels. The M20 has 20 cells and the M40 has 40. It supports Grade 1, Grade 2, Computer Braille and all 256 ASCII characters.
Predecessors: Braille Lite 40 (Blazie Engineering, Summer 1996)
