Braille Lite 18
Company: Blazie Engineering
Category: Notetakers
Released: Summer 1993

Overview:
A Braille 'n Speak with an 18-cell refreshable braille display.

Detailed Description:
The Braille Lite 18 is a small, flat, rectangular box of tan plastic, about 21.5 cm wide, 13 cm deep and 4.5 cm thick (roughly 8¼ by 5 by 2 inches). It weighs about 900 g with its battery, a bit heavier than a hardcover book. Four rubber feet on the bottom keep it from sliding on a desk.
On top is a seven-button braille keyboard in brown plastic, laid out like a Perkins brailler: six dot keys plus a space bar. The raised letters 'Braille Lite' and 'BLAZIE ENGINEERING' are molded into the top surface.
In a slightly recessed area below the keyboard, toward the front edge, is the 18-cell refreshable braille display, with eight-dot cells whose pins rise and fall. Just above the display runs an 'advance bar' that you press to move on to the next 18 characters.
The right side has the power toggle switch and a small headphone jack for private listening to the built-in speech. The left side has a 25-pin female parallel port for a printer and two round 8-pin mini-DIN jacks for serial connections to a computer or disk drive. A label on the bottom reads 'MADE IN USA, MODEL BLT-18, BLAZIE ENGINEERING' with a serial number.
It came in a black vinyl and nylon carrying case with a Velcro flap, a zippered pocket inside the flap, and a nylon shoulder strap clipped to plastic D-rings. In Japan it also came with a charging adapter, a serial cable, an earphone and manuals. A charge took about two hours and lasted about 30 hours of use.

History:
Blazie Engineering of Forest Hill, Maryland introduced the Braille Lite 18 (model BLT-18) in summer 1993. The APH Museum calls it basically an updated Braille 'n Speak 640 with an added 18-cell refreshable braille display. It worked as a notetaker with a clock, stopwatch, timer, calculator and notes, and could connect to a PC as a braille display. In Japan, Access Technology Inc. sold it with Japanese braille and English manuals.
Predecessors: Braille 'n Speak (original) (Blazie Engineering, 1987 to 1990s)
Successors: Braille Lite 40 (Blazie Engineering, Summer 1996); Braille Lite M20 / M40 (Freedom Scientific, 2002)
