ClassMate Reader
Company: HumanWare

Detailed Description:
The ClassMate Reader is a handheld reader about the size of a portable game console. It measures 157 × 91 × 30 mm (6.2 × 3.6 × 1.2 in) and weighs 250 g (8.8 oz) with its battery. The front is mostly a colour LCD touchscreen, operated with a stylus that stores in the right edge.
Down the left side of the screen, from top to bottom, are Escape, Study Tools, Home, Dictionary and Record, with the microphone above Study Tools. On the right are a four-way arrow pad with Enter in the centre, a Go To Page/Heading button and a Play button, with the speaker above the Up arrow. Holding Home for two seconds turns on Key Describer mode, which announces each button as you press it.
On the left edge, from back to front, are the headphone jack, a Volume/Speed up-and-down rocker and a Volume/Speed toggle button. On the right edge are the Power button near the top corner, an SD card slot in the centre and the built-in stylus.
The top edge has a mini-USB port on the left, used for computer transfers and for USB memory sticks and book cartridges, and the power input jack on the right. The back holds a battery door, secured with a screw, with a retractable stand around it.
It uses a 3.7 V, 2 Ah lithium-ion battery that gives about 7 hours of DAISY playback over headphones and takes 4 to 5 hours to charge. It reads SD and SDHC cards up to 8 GB, records voice notes, and came with earbuds, a carrying pouch and two USB cables.

History:
HumanWare introduced the ClassMate Reader as a revolutionary device for people with reading difficulties. It was a handheld reader for students and adults that played the audio of textbooks while highlighting the text on screen, to help with comprehension, reading speed and vocabulary. Revision 9 of its user guide is dated February 2, 2009. It played DAISY, NISO Z39.86 and NIMAS books, Bookshare.org text, Audible books, podcasts and music. Its built-in dictionary was based on the 2004 Oxford American Minidictionary, and it read text aloud with Nuance Vocalizer. Recording for the Blind & Dyslexic sold a $20 authorization key that unlocked its protected books.
