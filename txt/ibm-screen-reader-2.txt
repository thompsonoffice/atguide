History:
IBM announced it on June 23, 1992 (Announcement Letter 292-363), with availability planned for September 8, 1992. It was $600, or $500 as an upgrade from Screen Reader 1.x. IBM called it the first OS/2 access product for blind users and the first to speak text from IBM's graphical user interface. It ran in all OS/2 2.0 sessions, followed the visual focus, read menus automatically, could emulate mouse point-and-click and named icons. It came with ready-made profiles and a full profiling language and compiler. It needed the IBM Screen Reader Keypad, which plugged into the mouse port on IBM PS systems or into a Screen Reader Adapter card on other computers, and a non-IBM speech synthesizer. Messages and documentation were translated into twelve languages, and installation instructions came in print, audio, online and braille.
Predecessors: IBM Screen Reader (DOS) (IBM, 1986)
