Detailed Description:
The SMART Brailler is a Perkins-style manual braillewriter with an electronic Smart Module on the front. APH sold it as catalog no. 1-00820-00. Perkins lists it at 8.5 lb (3.9 kg) and 14 × 5.5 × 9.5 in (34 × 15 × 24 cm). It still embosses normally with the power off, and you carry it by a rounded Easy-Grip handle below the keys.
Across the front are nine keys: Line Spacing at far left, then dots 3, 2 and 1, the space bar, dots 4, 5 and 6, and Backspace at far right. Flat wing-shaped paper feed knobs stick out from each side, and two paper release levers sit at the top near the back. The carriage has a triangular release button and an oval Easy-Erase button just right of the embossing head.
The hinged Smart Module tilts forward. In the middle is a roughly 4-inch colour screen that shows SimBraille or large print. To its left are four Quick Buttons, marked with 0 to 3 raised lines (screen on/off, display mode, contracted or uncontracted braille, speech mode), and a speaker. To the right are a round green Menu button with a raised dot and a green diamond Select button, marked with a raised checkmark, surrounded by four navigation buttons.
The headphone jack is on top of the module at the right end, the two volume buttons are high on its right side, and a USB-A port is in a cutout lower on that side, for flash drives or a printer. Behind the module is a slot with left and right margin guides, with 36 raised cell markings above it. A bell sounds at cell 21 and again near the right margin.
A round rocker power switch is on the lower right side, and the power port is at the lower left of the back. The removable lithium-ion battery sits behind a two-screw door underneath and lasts about 5 hours. It embosses 25 lines of up to 28 cells on 8½ × 11 in paper and speaks with Acapela voices; Duxbury provides contracted-braille translation.

History:
Perkins launched a lighter, quieter Next Generation brailler for students in 2008. It won a 2009 International Design Excellence Award but got a mixed response from braille users, and Wikipedia says it was replaced by the SMART Brailler in 2012. Wikipedia notes that the original brailler's design itself remains unchanged.
Predecessors: Perkins Brailler (Perkins School for the Blind / Perkins Products, 1951 to present)
