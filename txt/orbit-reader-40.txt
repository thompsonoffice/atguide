Detailed Description:
The Orbit Reader 40 is a 40-cell braille display and note-taker from Orbit Research. It is a flat bar 11.6 inches long, 3.8 inches deep and 1.3 inches thick, weighing 1.65 pounds (750 grams). Orbit Research calls the construction solid and resistant to fluids and dust.
Place it with the Spacebar nearest you. Behind the Spacebar is the line of 40 cells, with a cursor-routing button behind each cell. At each end of the line is a rectangular rocker key that you press toward or away from you to pan.
Behind the routing buttons is a Perkins-style keyboard. From left to right are the keys for dots 7, 3, 2 and 1, then a four-way navigation pad with a Select button in its center, then dots 4, 5, 6 and 8. The front panel has two rectangular slots near each end for attaching a case or strap.
The left side has a USB-A port for thumb drives. The right side has, from front to back, the Power button, the USB-C port for charging and computer connection, and a 3.5 mm headphone jack. The SD card slot is on the back edge.
Underneath, each corner has a rubber foot. There are several screws, and a compartment for the user-replaceable battery. No case comes in the box. Orbit Research sold a padded nylon zip case with a top pocket for $59.95; a leather case was also offered.

History:
Scott Davert of the Helen Keller National Center reviewed it in October 2021. Unlike the Orbit Reader 20, it added cursor-routing buttons, built-in braille translation and connections to up to five Bluetooth devices. It has no internal memory: files live on an SD card (4 to 32 GB) or a USB drive. He estimated battery life at 20 to 22 hours of heavy use and found the cells quieter than the Orbit Reader 20's but the keyboard louder. The user-replaceable battery cost $44.95 at the time and $49.95 by January 2023.
Predecessors: Orbit Reader 20 (Orbit Research, Announced March 2016, shipped 2017)
Successors: Orbit Reader Q40 (Orbit Research, Announced March 19, 2024; shipping from June 2024)
