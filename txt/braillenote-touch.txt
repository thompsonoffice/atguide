BrailleNote Touch
Company: HumanWare
Category: Notetakers
Released: 2016

Overview:
The first Google-certified braille tablet with Play Store access, running KeySoft on Android.

Specs:
Released: 2016
Processor: TI OMAP4460 dual-core Cortex-A9 at 1.2 GHz
Memory (RAM): 2 GB
Storage: 32 GB internal SD (spec page); SD card up to 32 GB
Operating system: Android 4.4 with KeySoft
Default speech synthesizer: Acapela (default); Android TTS engines such as Vocalizer and CereProc can be added
Battery: 5,100 mAh, user-replaceable
Display or braille cells: 18 or 32 cells
Speech synthesizers available: Android text-to-speech engines (KeySoft on Android)
Price at launch: 18 cells $3,995; 32 cells $5,495 (2016)
Used price today (eBay): Not documented

Physical Description:
24.4 x 16.2 x 2.06 cm, 900 g (2 lb).

Detailed Description:
The BrailleNote Touch is an Android braille tablet launched at CSUN in 2016. The tablet itself is 9½ inches wide, 6.3 inches deep and 0.8 inch high and weighs 2 pounds (900 g). In its case with the keyboard it is about 10.2 × 6.9 × 1.6 inches and 3 pounds.
Most of the top is a glass touch screen with a 7-inch visual display that can be turned off. You can type braille right on the glass with TouchBraille: rest all ten fingers on the screen, feel a short vibration, and the virtual keys find your fingers.
The carrying case has a physical Perkins-style braille keyboard built in. It folds over the glass, held in place by magnets, and plugs into a port on the back of the tablet; fold it back to use the touch screen.
Nearest you is an 18- or 32-cell braille display with a routing button over each cell and a stereo speaker at each end. On the front edge are four thumb keys, two short and two long, with three small keys between them: a triangular Back key, a round Home key and a square key for the context menu or app switcher.
On the left side are a micro USB port, an oval power button and volume buttons. The back has a full-size USB port, an SD card slot and an HDMI port (video only). The right side has microphone and headphone jacks and a camera button. Under the tablet, two latches release the replaceable battery and there is a camera window.

History:
The BrailleNote line began in 2000 with Pulse Data's Windows CE-based Classic, followed by the mPower series (2005) and the Apex (2009). The BrailleNote Touch (2016) moved the line to Android, and the BrailleNote Touch Plus followed in 2019. In 2025 HumanWare previewed the Evolve, which moves the line again, to Windows 11.
Predecessors: BrailleNote Apex BT32 (HumanWare, November 2009)
Successors: BrailleNote Touch Plus (HumanWare, 2019)
