Detailed Description:
The BookSense is HIMS's pocket book and audio player, sold in the US by GW Micro from 2009. It reads DAISY books (NLS, Bookshare, Audible), text and Word files, plays music and records. The standard model cost $349.
AccessWorld compares it to a candy-bar cell phone, 4.25 × 1.85 × 0.73 inches and 4 ounces, the smallest of the players it tested. A lanyard attaches at the top center.
The front, from the top: stereo speakers, a raised date-and-time button, a five-way control with nibs on the four arrows and a small square Menu button in the center, then a 3-by-4 phone keypad. The keypad has a nib on 5, and alternate keys are slightly concave or convex.
On the right side are the Play/Pause button (hold it for power), a Record button and a key-lock switch. On the left side are the Mode button, volume up and down and the SD card slot. The top edge has the microphone and 3.5 mm headphone jacks, the bottom the AC jack and USB port, and the back the battery compartment.
It reads with NeoSpeech Paul and Kate voices. A user report gives about 12 hours per charge and 2.5 hours to recharge. Holding the clock button turns on voice commands.

History:
HIMS, maker of the BrailleSense, built the BookSense, and GW Micro (maker of Window-Eyes) sold it in the US. AccessWorld reviewed it alongside the Victor Reader Stream and PLEXTALK Pocket. The standard model cost $349 and was the smallest of the three, shaped like a candy-bar phone at 4.25 by 1.85 by 0.73 inches and 4 ounces. It used NeoSpeech Paul and Kate voices and played NLS, Bookshare, Audible and DAISY books.
Successors: BookSense XT (HIMS / Selvas BLV, 2009); BookSense DS (HIMS / Selvas BLV, 2010)
