Detailed Description:
The Orbit Speak is a pocket note-taker from Orbit Research, with a braille keyboard and speech but no braille display. It is a small bar about 6.5 inches long, under 3 inches wide and 0.6 inch thick, about the weight of a smartphone. Orbit Research gives 180 grams; the NFB's review gives 210 grams.
Place it on a desk. At the back of the top surface is a six-key Perkins-style keyboard with quiet scissor keys. Between dots 1 and 4 is a navigation pad of up, down, left and right arrows around a Select button.
Directly in front of the pad is the spacebar. Immediately left and right of the spacebar are the keys for dots 7 and 8, used for Backspace and Enter and pressed with the thumbs, which keeps the unit narrow. Beyond them are function keys: F1 and F2 on the left, F3 and F4 on the right.
The left side has a 3.5 mm headphone jack and the power button, which has a distinctive raised nub. Holding it from a full shutdown gives a series of vibrations; a tap puts it into hibernation and a second tap wakes it where you left off. The right side has a USB-C port for charging and accessories, including USB audio devices.
The SD card slot is on the front edge. It has a built-in speaker and vibration feedback.

History:
Jonathan Mosen's review for the NFB's Access On podcast notes that it answered calls for a modern Braille 'n Speak and predates the return of the Blazie brand. It is braille in and speech out, with no braille display. The standard and Plus models are physically identical. The Plus adds a music player and more, and upgrading later costs $50 more than buying the Plus at first. Its menus use chords from as far back as the VersaBraille of the 1980s.
