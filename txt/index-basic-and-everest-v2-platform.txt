Detailed Description:
Index Braille's version 2 platform (V2) covered the Basic single-sided embosser and the Everest sheet-fed interpoint embosser. Braillecast dates it to 1995, and SAST's identification guide gives production as February 1995 to 2002.
SAST says the V2 Basic has a red lid on a black case. Every V2 printed through a Centronics parallel interface only. The serial port on V2 and V3 machines is for service and cannot be used for embossing, and SAST adds that USB-to-parallel leads generally do not work well.
No V2 spec sheet turned up in this research. The nearest source is the 1994 NFB/APH Braille Technology Evaluation, which tested the models sold just before V2. It gives the Basic as 20 × 11.75 × 7.5 in, 14 lb, 50 characters per second and $2,995. Its paper fed from front to back across the top, with four switches and an impression knob at the rear left of the top plate, and serial and parallel ports, a power switch and the power cord on the back.
That report gives the Everest as 20 in wide, 6.75 in deep and 25 in tall, weighing 25 lb, rated at 100 characters per second and priced at $5,995. A feeder on top held 100 to 150 single sheets, which passed down through the printer into a catch basket at the bottom. The front-panel buttons had braille labels and spoke through built-in speech, and a cut-down Duxbury translator was built into ROM.
These measurements describe the pre-1995 models, not the V2 machines, and are given only as the closest documented reference. SAST says Index stopped supporting V2 embossers on January 1, 2011.

History:
A UK service note dates V2 production from February 1995 to 2002. The Basic had a red lid on a black case, and the only interface was parallel; the serial port was for service only. Index says the Basic V2 already looked much like today's Basic. Support for V2 ended on January 1, 2011.
Predecessors: Index Everest-D (V1) (Index Braille, 1992)
Successors: Index V3 platform (Index Braille, 2002 to 2011)
