Detailed Description:
The TeleSensory Navigator is a low, flat, rectangular refreshable braille display. It came in 20-cell six-dot, 40-cell six- or eight-dot, and 80-cell eight-dot models, priced from $4,500 to $16,000 in 1994. Some models could run on batteries.
The 40-cell six-dot model that the NFB evaluated measures 12 inches wide by 12.5 inches deep by 1.75 inches high and weighs 5 pounds 11 ounces.
Ten buttons on the front panel form two mirror-image groups of five. Each group has two long horizontal up and down buttons in the middle, a vertical left and right button on either side, and a smaller button to the side. The smaller buttons act as Enter, link or unlink the display from the cursor, or start View2, the real-time braille translator. Mostly, the right-hand group moves the reading window and the left-hand group moves the system cursor.
Between the two button groups is a jack like an RJ-11 phone jack for connecting a BrailleMate. Its braille keyboard can then enter six- or eight-dot braille, and the display can be split between the BrailleMate and the computer.
On the back are the input and output connectors, an on/off switch and the power cord. It connects to the PC through a serial port and runs TeleSensory's Gateway software, with a TRACK utility for following alternate cursors and the VBPC VersaBraille emulator.

History:
The NFB timeline dates the Navigator to 1989 as TSI's first dedicated refreshable braille display for PC access. It came in 20-cell (six-dot desktop), 40-cell (eight-dot) and 80-cell versions, sat below the PC keyboard and had cursor routing keys. The PowerBraille line replaced it in the mid-1990s.
