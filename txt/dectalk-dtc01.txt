DECtalk DTC01
Company: Digital Equipment Corporation (DEC)
Category: Speech Synthesizers
Released: 1984

Overview:
DEC's hardware synthesizer, based on Dennis Klatt's research. Its default voice was Perfect Paul.

Detailed Description:
The DTC01 is a large desktop cabinet. Wikipedia lists it at about 18 x 12 x 4 inches (45.7 x 30.5 x 10.2 cm) and 16 pounds (7.2 kg); Trace's 1996-97 ResourceBook gives a weight of 19 pounds. The Computer History Museum's example has a front that slopes back at the top, with ventilation slits in both sides.
The front carries only an on/off switch. According to the Computer History Museum's description, everything else is on the back, from left to right: two DB-25 male connectors (one labelled COMM, the other apparently marked with a monitor icon), two RJ-11 telephone jacks (one marked with a handset icon), two audio jacks (marked with a headphone icon and a triangle), a green LED, a volume thumbwheel, and the AC inlet with a fuse and a 120/240-volt selector.
DEC's owner's manual says the unit has a built-in loudspeaker and volume control. Plugging headphones or an auxiliary speaker into the headphone jack on the back turns the internal speaker off; the separate audio output jack, meant for an amplifier, leaves the internal speaker on, so you silence it by turning the volume control down.
The two telephone jacks let DECtalk sit between a phone line and a telephone set, so it could answer and place calls; the manual lists USOC RJ11C (or RJXA1) as the line jack type, model DTC01-AA, ringer equivalence 0.3B. A host computer connects to the COMM port over RS-232C, and a terminal can be connected to the second port for setting up the unit.
At power-on the unit runs a self-test of about seven seconds and then says 'DECtalk version ... is running', the only start-up confirmation besides the green LED. Trace's ResourceBook describes nine voices (four male, four female, one child) and speech through the internal speaker, headphones, an external speaker or the telephone.

History:
DEC announced the DTC01 in December 1983 and delivered the first units in February 1984. It connected over RS-232C and could also handle telephone touch tones. Dennis Klatt began looking for an industrial partner for his MIT work in 1981. Larger quantities shipped in March 1984. The units had two telephone jacks, one for the phone line and one for a handset, so DECtalk could answer and place calls, read email aloud, and run touch-tone alert systems. Uses ranged from public information services to giving a voice to people with speech disabilities. Later versions came on ISA cards for PCs and as software: DECtalk Software for Digital UNIX and for Windows NT, and DECtalk Access32.
