Detailed Description:
The VoiceSense is a speech-only notetaker from HIMS with a braille keyboard and no braille display. GW Micro's published specifications gave its size as 18.8 by 7.7 by 2.5 centimeters (about 7.4 by 3 by 1 inch) and its weight as 266 grams, just over half a pound.
The top carries a Perkins-style braille keyboard. The sources found do not describe the remaining controls in detail.
For sound it has stereo speakers and a headphone jack. For input it has a built-in condenser microphone and a microphone jack, used for recording, and there is an FM radio receiver. The sources checked don't say where on the case the jacks are.
Its ports are USB On-The-Go (USB 2.0 compatible), an SD card slot and a CompactFlash slot. Wireless is 802.11b/g Wi-Fi and Bluetooth 1.1. Inside is a 540 MHz Intel PXA270 processor with 128 MB of RAM, 64 MB of OS storage and 1 GB of user flash, running Windows CE .NET 5.0.
A detachable, user-replaceable lithium-polymer battery gives about 12 hours of continuous use. 2007 news reports put the price at about $1,800 to $2,150. The later VoiceSense QWERTY used the smooth white plastic shell of other HIMS products, and at CSUN 2010 GW Micro's Dan Weirich dropped one on the floor and kept using it.

History:
HIMS's product history lists only two VoiceSense models: the original (2007) and the QWERTY (2010). GW Micro distributed it in the US. A user quoting GW Micro's spec page in October 2007 listed the hardware, and Sendero GPS was due for it within weeks. After HIMS and GW Micro split in 2011, HIMS sold it directly from Austin, Texas, alongside a QWERTY VoiceSense.
Successors: VoiceSense QWERTY (HIMS / Selvas BLV, 2010)
