Detailed Description:
The b.note is a braille display and notetaker from Eurobraille, made with 20 or 40 eight-dot cells. The 40-cell version is a long, slim bar 365 millimetres wide, 105 deep and 27 high, weighing 600 grams; the 20-cell version is 235 millimetres wide and 500 grams. A protective bumper wraps the case, and a zippered case comes with it.
The braille cells run across the top. Above each cell is a routing key with two contacts: a light press moves the display forward or back, so you need not hunt for a scroll key, and a firm press routes the cursor.
Eight braille keys sit in a row above the routing keys, with two thumb keys below the display, ten keys in all. On the standard and basic versions they are a Perkins-style keyboard; the light versions have none. At each end of the display is a keypad of four buttons; the up key on the left keypad opens the b.note menu.
On the left edge are a small reset hole for a paper clip, the USB-C port for charging and computers, a 3.5 mm headphone jack and the key lock switch. The power button is a pull-to-activate lock: a short press puts it in transport mode and a long press of more than 2 seconds in standby. On the second generation, a USB-A port on the back takes memory sticks.
Underneath, a screwed panel covers the SD card, for service only. On the second generation, two grilles at the front let out the sound of two built-in speakers.

History:
Eurobraille sells it in standard, basic, light and basic/light versions, with or without the Perkins keyboard and Bluetooth/internal apps. Built-in functions include a file explorer, editor (docx, odt, pdf, epub, brf, txt), MP3 player, web radio and podcasts, agenda, EOLE library reader, Wikipedia access, a braille learning mode and games. A second generation adds two speakers and a USB-A port. The free b.view app shows the display to sighted companions. The 40-cell model was listed as out of stock on Eurobraille's site in October 2026.
