Orca versions
Company: GNOME Project
Category: Operating system or software version history
Versions listed: 7

Overview:
Orca is the GNOME desktop's free screen reader, written in Python. Marc Mulcahy, a blind programmer at Sun Microsystems, built the first prototype in May 2004. Sun's Accessibility Program Office, led by Willie Walker, carried it to v1.0 in GNOME 2.16 (September 2006), replacing Gnopernicus. After Oracle bought Sun, Walker was laid off (early 2010) and volunteers led by Joanmarie Diggs took over. Igalia hired Diggs on September 7, 2011. Orca follows GNOME's six-month release cycle.

Version History:

Version 1: Prototype
Released: May 2004
Notes: First prototype by Marc Mulcahy at Sun.

Version 2: 0.x development releases
Released: 2004 to 2006
Notes: Early test releases, ending with v0.9.0.

Version 3: 1.0.0
Released: September 3, 2006
Notes: First official release, in GNOME 2.16. It targeted the GNOME desktop, yelp, gedit, gnome-terminal, Evolution 2.8, gaim and OpenOffice 2.0.4/StarOffice, with updated guides, speech-engine fallback and Java/OpenOffice fixes. Shipped in Solaris, Ubuntu and Fedora.

Version 4: 2.17.x
Released: Late 2006 to early 2007
Notes: Development series with 'significant usability improvements' over 1.0.

Version 5: 1.0.1
Released: January 28, 2007
Notes: Translation-only release for GNOME 2.16.3.

Version 6: Volunteer era
Released: 2010 onward
Notes: After Oracle's layoffs, led by Joanmarie Diggs; supported by Igalia from September 2011.

Version 7: 48.1
Released: May 1, 2025
Notes: Stable release listed on Wikipedia; Orca now uses GNOME version numbers.

Devices and Software That Ran It:
Orca (GNOME Project, 2006 to present)

System Requirements:
Linux or another Unix-like OS with GNOME's AT-SPI accessibility stack, Python, and a speech engine through Speech Dispatcher (eSpeak NG by default on most distributions); BRLTTY for braille.
