Detailed Description:
The first-generation Ray-Ban Meta glasses look and feel like ordinary Ray-Ban frames, such as the square Wayfarer. The Wayfarer comes in Standard (48 grams, 131 mm hinge to hinge) and Large (50 grams, 137 mm) sizes. There's no screen; everything is heard through two small open-ear speakers in the arms, aimed at your ears, so you still hear the world around you. Five microphones are spread across the frame: two in each arm and one near the nose pad.
At the front corners of the frame are two small round openings. One is the 12-megapixel ultra-wide camera; the other (on the left, per Ray-Ban) is a capture light that glows to tell people nearby when you take a photo or video. Inside, near the right arm's hinge, is a notification light that only you see.
The right arm has two controls. Along its outer side is a touchpad: tap once to pause or play, double-tap to skip forward or answer a call, triple-tap to skip back, swipe forward or back for volume, and press and hold to talk to Meta AI or reject a call. On top of the frame is the capture button: press once for a photo, press and hold for video.
The power switch is a small slider on the inner side of the left arm, near the hinge; slide it toward the lens to turn on and back to turn off. The product name is printed inside the right arm, and the serial number inside the left arm. The glasses are IPX4 splash resistant, so light rain is fine but they shouldn't be submerged.
The glasses charge only in their hard case, through metal contacts at the nose bridge. The case has a USB-C port for charging, a light on the front (orange while charging, green when full) and a pairing button on the back. Glasses last up to about 4 hours of moderate use and charge fully in about 75 minutes; the case holds up to 32 hours more. The box includes the glasses, the case and a cleaning cloth.

History:
Meta and Ray-Ban launched Ray-Ban Stories in September 2021 and announced the second-generation Ray-Ban Meta on September 27, 2023, with 12-megapixel cameras and Meta AI. In April 2024, Meta AI on the glasses gained camera-based visual input. On November 13, 2024, Be My Eyes became available on them, first in the US, Canada, UK, Ireland and Australia: saying "Hey Meta, Be My Eyes" connects you to a sighted volunteer who sees through the glasses' camera and talks to you through the open-ear speakers.
