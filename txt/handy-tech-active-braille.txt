Detailed Description:
The Active Braille (as described in Help Tech's version 4.5 manual, 2018) is a slim 40-cell braille display with built-in note-taking. It measures about 12.6 inches wide, 4.9 inches deep and 1.1 inches high, and weighs about 2 pounds (930 grams).
Place it with the keys toward you. Along the front edge, in the center, are two space bars. Above them, in one row across the unit, are eight braille input keys arranged like a Perkins keyboard: from left to right, dots 7, 3, 2, 1, 4, 5, 6 and 8.
The middle of the top holds 40 concave piezoelectric braille cells. Each cell has its own built-in cursor routing key, so there is no separate row of routing buttons. At each end of the braille line, flush with the cells, is a Triple Action Key: a rocker that you press at its top to read backward, at its bottom to read forward, or in its center for extra commands.
On the right side, from front to back, are the main power button, a USB-A port for a keyboard, and the power socket. On the left side, from front to back, are a second USB-A keyboard port, the microSD card slot, and the USB-B port for connecting to a computer. On the back, at the left, is a 2.5 mm headphone or speaker jack, and a 3.5 mm adapter cable is included.
It has 4 GB of storage and Bluetooth. Help Tech rates the battery at about 20 hours, or about 14 hours when using Bluetooth, with a quick charge in under 3 hours. Its ATC (Active Tactile Control) technology senses where your fingers are reading on the display.

History:
Handy Tech introduced the Active Braille in 2011 with its ATC (Active Tactile Control) technology, first used in 2006 on the Modular Evolution. Help Tech released a more compact redesign with improved controls in 2020–21.
