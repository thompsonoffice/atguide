Detailed Description:
The Smart Beetle is a 14-cell braille display from HIMS with no notetaking applications, only a menu and a terminal clipboard. AppleVis's reviewer found it about the size of an iPhone 6S but a little thicker and wider, almost exactly the size of a Focus 14. It is a rectangle with rounded edges.
With the braille line toward you, the 14 cells run along the front of the top surface, with a diamond-shaped panning button at each end. Directly behind the cells are 14 cursor-routing buttons.
Behind those, on a slightly raised level, is the space bar with two rectangular keys on each side: F1 and F2 on the left, F3 and F4 on the right. Behind them is the eight-key Perkins-style keyboard. At the very back of the top are a small speaker for audio alerts and a couple of indicator lights.
The back panel has a reset button at the far left and a micro-USB port on the right. The left side and front are bare. The power button is alone on the right side: a quick press opens the menu, which offers Bluetooth serial, USB or Bluetooth keyboard modes, and a three-second press turns it on or off. Pairing with iOS needs no PIN.
It connects to up to five Bluetooth devices plus one USB device at once, and battery life is about 18 hours. It comes with a thin, close-fitting case that closes magnetically, a micro-USB cable, print and braille guides, and a driver CD. It listed at $1,345 and was on sale for $995 in early 2018.

History:
Scott Davert's AppleVis review (December 2015) gave it 4 stars. He noted that it was natively supported in iOS 9 and OS X El Capitan, while earlier iOS versions treated it as an 18-cell BrailleSense, so panning skipped text. AccessWorld (2018) found that it had not been updated in a long time, and that the manual's keyboard-emulation section was incomplete.
