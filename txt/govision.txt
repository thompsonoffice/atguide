Detailed Description:
The GoVision is a one-piece, foldable desktop video magnifier. It combines a 24-inch widescreen LCD with a camera on a movable arm, and it has a carrying handle. Unfolded, it is about 57 cm (22.4 inches) wide and 55 cm (21.7 inches) tall, and 19 cm (7.5 inches) deep with the camera folded. It weighs 7.4 kg (about 16.3 pounds), according to CERTAM.
To set it up, you unfold the camera arm and the stand. The camera rotates through 260 degrees: it can point down at a page on the desk, out toward a distant board, or back at your face as a mirror. A rocker button on the camera itself changes zoom and colour modes and switches the LED lights on and off. The screen tilts but cannot be raised, swivelled or turned to portrait.
You control it with a wired 14-key remote whose buttons can be told apart by shape, relief, colour and symbol. The top keys set zoom, colours, contrast, and reading lines or masks. The centre keys open menus and navigate. The bottom keys freeze the image, start OCR and speech, and open the file manager for the USB stick. A headphone jack is on the top edge of the remote.
Every command is shown briefly on screen and also spoken; for example, the current zoom level is announced. Near-view magnification runs from 1.8 to 70 times. Up to five users can save their own settings.
Connections include one USB port, an HDMI input for showing a computer, tablet or phone on the screen, and a VGA connector. It runs on a 12-volt, 3-amp power adapter and takes about 35 seconds to start. In the box are the remote, an HDMI cable, headphones, a USB stick, a large-print quick-start guide and a laminated reference card, with an X-Y reading table as an option.

History:
HIMS of Korea designed the GoVision with Visiole, its French distributor. France's Association Valentin Haüy (CERTAM) lists its release as November 2016 at €3,500, and the magnifier won a Silmo d'Or low-vision award. CERTAM's January 2017 evaluation, of firmware 1.1.0, found it a complete HD magnifier for near, distance and mirror (self) viewing. It has Android-based firmware, OCR with speech in 18 languages, and playback of documents, PDFs, images and videos from a USB stick. CERTAM's criticisms were that OCR captured only the visible screen area, was slow, and made errors at low zoom.
Successors: GoVision Pro (HIMS / Selvas BLV, 2018)
