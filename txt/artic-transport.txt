Detailed Description:
Artic's fall 1992 Visions newsletter gives TransPort's size as 0.75 inch high, 4.125 inches wide and 7.5 inches long, and its weight as about 15 ounces, small enough for a coat pocket. Trace's 1996-97 ResourceBook lists slightly different figures, 4 x 7 x 1.4 inches and 1.25 pounds; the source of the difference (a later case or a different measurement) is not stated.
The case is aluminium. Volume and tone are set with two thumbwheels rather than knobs, which makes them easy to find and turn by touch.
Sound comes from an internal speaker, or from headphones or an external speaker plugged into the unit. The computer connects through a serial port; Trace notes it works with the IBM PC and PS/2 families.
TransPort runs on rechargeable batteries or AC power and can be used while charging. Artic quoted about 16 hours per charge and built in a low-battery alarm. The 615's battery-backed memory kept stored files for about thirty days after the unit was switched off with a full charge.
The flagship model 615 has an extra Turbo Port for Artic's Gizmo navigation pad or Turbo Pedals, and comes with a Turbo Pad that plugs into the front of the unit. The model 611 is the same unit without Business Vision in ROM.

History:
Artic announced TransPort in its Visions newsletter in fall 1992, after years of users asking for a portable, battery-powered synthesizer as responsive as its internal cards. TransPort was 0.75 inch high, 4.125 inches wide and 7.5 inches long, and weighed about 15 ounces. It had an aluminum case, used the Artic 263 speech chip, had thumbwheels for volume and tone, and could use its built-in speaker, headphones or an external speaker. It ran about 16 hours on rechargeable batteries, warned when the battery was low, and could be used while charging. The flagship model 615 included Artic's Business Vision screen reader, which could be copied from its ROM disk to any PC; the VEST speech rules; a clock with 16 alarms; battery-backed file storage; and a port for Artic's Gizmo pad or Turbo Pedals. The model 611 was the same without Business Vision. Both could emulate Artic's own PortTalk codes or the external Accent synthesizer, so other screen readers could drive them. Later came the palm-sized Mini TransPort 621 and 622; all are now out of production.
