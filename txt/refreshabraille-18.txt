Detailed Description:
The Refreshabraille 18 is APH's 18-cell Bluetooth braille display and keyboard. Its footprint is about the size of an index card, roughly 3 by 5 inches, and it weighs about 11 ounces. It is housed in a sturdy metal case.
Hold it with the display furthest from you. There are 18 eight-dot piezoelectric cells, with a cursor-routing button below each. Below those are three evenly spaced rectangular buttons.
Next is a straight row of six braille keys. Centered below them, where a spacebar might be, is a round joystick, and directly below that is the spacebar itself. On either side of the spacebar is a key for dot 7 or dot 8. Because the unit is narrow, these sit below the other keys and are pressed with the thumbs.
The power button is centered on the front edge, flush with the case, so the outline stays smooth.
The mini-USB port is in a cut-out on the bottom, so a plugged-in cable lies under the display, protected.

History:
Deborah Kendrick reviewed it for AccessWorld in July 2010. It works as both a braille display and a braille keyboard. It shipped with a USB cable, a CD of documentation and drivers, and quick-start sheets in print and braille, but no AC adapter. When powered off, all dots stand up; a press of about two seconds on Power vibrates it and shows APH and the serial number.
