Detailed Description:
The Type 'n Speak is Blazie Engineering's typewriter-keyboard sibling of the Braille 'n Speak. Deane Blazie said it was built for people who liked the Braille 'n Speak but did not know braille. The manual calls it a laptop-style computer with a built-in speech synthesizer that speaks every prompt and everything you type.
Physically it is a flat box about a foot wide, seven inches from front to back and one inch high, weighing about two pounds with its internal rechargeable battery. It has no display screen at all. The whole top surface is a laptop-style QWERTY keyboard.
Set it down with the spacebar nearest you. About halfway back along the right edge is the on/off rocker switch. Rock it away from you to turn the unit on and toward you to turn it off.
Many keys are renamed for the Type 'n Speak. The top-right key (Break on a PC) is Help, and Shift+Help enters Learn mode, which names each key without acting on it. Down the right side, Home becomes Top, PgUp and PgDn jump by paragraph, and End becomes Bottom. The arrow keys sit in the bottom-right corner.
The manual says it stores about 300 print pages in RAM (758 KB) and about 800 more in 2 MB of flash memory. It serves as a notebook, address book, calendar, calculator, stopwatch and timer.
