History:
Jonathan Duddington started 'speak' for Acorn RISC OS in 1995 and rewrote it as eSpeak in 2007 with more languages. Development passed to eSpeak NG in 2015. Jonathan Duddington released Speak for RISC OS computers in 1995, supporting British English. Speak 1.05 came out under the GPLv2 on February 17, 2006, first for Linux, with a Windows SAPI 5 version in January 2007. After version 1.14 it was renamed eSpeak, and from 1.27 it used the GPLv3. It is a compact formant synthesizer covering many languages; many of the people who improved its languages were blind users. Google Translate used it for 27 languages in 2010. The last development release was 1.48.15, on April 16, 2015.
Successors: eSpeak NG (eSpeak / eSpeak NG project, Late 2015 to present)
