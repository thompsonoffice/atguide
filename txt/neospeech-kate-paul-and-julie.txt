History:
NeoSpeech later moved its TTS hosting to Amazon. NeoSpeech's Kate voice was released in September 2001 and Paul in May 2002. Kate was released in September 2001 as the first American English voice in VoiceText, from the Korean company Voiceware (founded 2000). In February 2002 Voiceware set up its US subsidiary NeoSpeech, founded by speech engineers Lin Chase and Yoon Kim, later based in Santa Clara, California. VoiceText uses unit selection synthesis, built from large databases of recorded speech. Stephen Hawking briefly used NeoSpeech in 2004 before returning to his DECtalk voice, and Adobe chose it for Captivate. ZoomText 9 offered NeoSpeech voices (Paul and one female US voice) as natural-sounding alternatives to its ViaVoice speech; reviewer Veli-Pekka Tätilä praised Paul's intonation but noted a lag before speech. Pentax bought Voiceware in January 2006, HOYA bought Pentax in 2007 to 2008, and after HOYA bought ReadSpeaker in 2017 it put NeoSpeech, Voiceware and VoiceText under the ReadSpeaker brand.
