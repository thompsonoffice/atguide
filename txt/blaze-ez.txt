A HIMS book player in the Blaze line. HIMS lists the Blaze EZ (2014) under 'DAISY Player and OCR Multi Player' in its product history, between the BookSense XT (2009) and the Blaze ET (2015).

Detailed Description:
The Blaze EZ is a pocket book player and reader from HIMS, about the size and shape of a smartphone. Its manual gives it as 59.3 millimeters wide, 116.9 long and 16.4 thick, and 118 grams; a reviewer weighed it at 138 grams. The case is white, with contrasting color on the Power, Media, Radio and Books buttons.
Hold it keypad up. At the top center is a slightly recessed square Power button, with a stereo speaker grille on each side. Below and to the left is a round Record button. Below that is a row of three square buttons with braille letters: M for Music or Media, R for Radio and B for Books.
Below them is a circular navigation pad of Up, Down, Left and Right with a small round Select button in the middle. Below that are three buttons: a square Cancel on the left, a small dot-like Explorer button in the middle, and a square OCR button marked with braille O on the right. At the very bottom are two status lights on the left and the microphone on the right.
The left side has the SD card slot in its lower part. The right side has a rotary volume control, a control for speed and voice guidance, and a lock key. The bottom edge has the headphone socket, the micro-USB charging port and a line-in socket. A small hole in the center of the top edge takes the lanyard.
On the back, near the top, is the camera lens. A removable cover on the back hides the flat rectangular battery. An accessory pack added a stand for OCR, a spare battery and charger, a remote and a charging cradle.

History:
Model T70EZ. Similar in size to its predecessor, the BookSense, but with fewer keys, it added a built-in camera for one-button OCR. It plays DAISY, NLS, Bookshare and Learning Ally books, music and podcasts, and has an FM radio and recorder. Jackie Brown's review for Sight and Sound found a 4.5-hour charge gave about 17 hours of use, liked the sound, but missed a phone-style keypad like the Victor Reader Stream's.
