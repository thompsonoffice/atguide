Detailed Description:
The Handy Tech Braille Wave is a very compact 40-cell braille display with a built-in notetaker. It stores up to 4 MB in as many as 512 files, which the HTCom software transfers to and from a PC. It ships in a carrying case with a Velcro flap and an adjustable shoulder strap.
Along the front edge are three keys: Escape on the left, a wide space bar in the middle and Enter on the right. Behind them is a row of eight braille-input keys in two groups of four, ordered 7, 3, 2, 1 and 4, 5, 6, 8 from left to right. They also act as function keys.
Behind the keys are 40 ergonomic braille cells with a cursor-routing key built into each cell. A back-reading key [L] sits flush with the cells at the left end, and a forward-reading key [R] at the right end.
On the right side, near the front, is the main power switch: flip it towards you to turn the unit on. Behind it is the power socket (9 V DC, 1.5 A). On the left side are a telephone-style serial port for the PC, with a round port behind it for an optional external keyboard. Bluetooth models add a small slide switch near the front of the left side that chooses between Bluetooth and serial.
It runs on a set of four NiMH batteries in two compartments in the base, which came with an insulating strip to remove before first use. Also in the box were two foam keyboard pads, the power supply and a braille quick reference. A free DOS screen reader, BW-PRO, was available. The manual's size and weight figures could not be read in the online copy.

History:
Handy Tech presented the Braille Wave in 1999 as the first braille system with note-taking capability, the same year it opened a consulting center in Marburg. In 2004, the Braille Wave was one of the Handy Tech systems equipped with Bluetooth.
