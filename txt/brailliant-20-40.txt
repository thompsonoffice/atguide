Detailed Description:
The original HumanWare Brailliant is a compact braille display in a light-metal case, built with the same techniques used for modern camera bodies. The guide lists models with 24, 32, 40, 64 and 80 cells. At the 2004 launch, HumanWare gave the weight of the 40-cell model as 560 g (1.23 lb) and the 24-cell model as 389 g (0.86 lb), and a BrailleNote mailing-list post in July 2004 gave US prices of $3,695 for the 24 and $5,895 for the 40.
The braille cells run along the front, with a small cursor-routing key behind each cell. Three navigation keys sit to the left of the cells and three to the right. They are numbered like braille dots: D1, D2 and D3 on the left (from back to front), and D4, D5 and D6 on the right. What they do depends on the screen reader.
The power button is slightly recessed at the back of the top-left side. Hold it for about 3 seconds to turn the display on or off, and it beeps to confirm.
On the left side are three connectors: a rectangular mini-USB port, a round 5-volt power socket in the middle, and a round serial port, shaped like a headset jack, on the right. Bluetooth has no external connector because the antenna is built in. A System Menu, opened with its own key combination and operated with the routing keys, sets the port, protocol and auto-power-off.
An internal battery charges fully in about 2.5 hours from the supplied adapter, or over USB. The guide gives about 25 to 30 hours on Bluetooth and up to 100 hours on USB or serial. It worked with VIRGO, Blindows, JAWS, Talks and Mobile Speak.

History:
HumanWare launched the Brailliant 20 and 40 in 2004. The NFB's 2026 Braille Technology Timeline calls them the first refreshable braille displays with Bluetooth wireless connectivity. The timeline also notes that BAUM made OEM versions of HumanWare's Brailliant and BrailleConnect displays in the 2000s, a partnership that ended in 2011. HumanWare has kept the Brailliant name for later displays, and its latest model adds notetaker, book-reading and audio functions.
Successors: Brailliant BI 40X (HumanWare, On sale by April 2021 (Helen Keller Services review))
