Optacon II
Company: Telesensory Systems (TSI)
Category: Other
Released: 1985 (developed with Canon)

Overview:
A redesigned Optacon developed by Telesensory with Canon Inc., with improved packaging and the ability to connect to a computer, so users could read computer screens.

Detailed Description:
The Optacon II is the second-generation Optacon, made by Telesensory with Canon in 1985. Like the original, it turns print seen by a small handheld camera into a vibrating tactile picture under one fingertip. Its main change was a computer interface for reading what is on a screen.
Its tactile array had 100 pins instead of the original 144. The change was made to cut cost, and sources say it made the device less usable and hurt sales.
Like every Optacon, it has two parts: a small handheld camera probe about the size of a penknife, and a main unit with the tactile pin array, joined by a thin cable. You slide the camera across a line of print with one hand while resting the index finger of the other hand on the array.
Its array is 20 rows by 5 columns of tiny metal rods that vibrate where the camera sees black. As the camera moves, letter shapes flow across your fingertip. Controls have traditionally been minimal: vibration strength, a black/white threshold and a polarity setting.
Canon redesigned the packaging. Special lens modules let the camera read typewriters, calculators and computer screens, and a dedicated computer interface let it read screen text directly. Long-time users largely kept their older 144-pin Optacons.

History:
In 1985 Canon Inc. and Telesensory cooperated on the Optacon II. To lower its cost, the tactile array was cut from 144 pins to 100, and that decision is cited as the reason the Optacon II was not commercially successful. It was discontinued with the rest of the Optacon line in 1996.
Predecessors: Optacon (Telesensory Systems (TSI), 1971 to 1996 (models included the R1C and R1D))
