Detailed Description:
The iBill is a small black talking money identifier from Orbit Research, about the size of a pack of chewing gum: 3 inches long, 1.6 inches wide and 0.7 inch thick, weighing about 1.5 ounces. It is made of rugged plastic.
On the second-generation model, one large face has an overlapping plastic guide fixed on top, offset slightly so it does not reach the right or top edges. This leaves an open platform where you slide in the narrow end of a bill.
There is a button at each end, either of which starts identification. Each is textured, marked by a raised box with a vertical bar, and slightly recessed so it is not pressed by accident in a pocket. Holding one and tapping the other cycles through vibration, tone and three speech volumes.
A tiny metal key ring is on one end. Almost at the corner beside it is a 2.5 mm headphone jack, which AccessWorld found very hard to find by touch until you know where it is.
The battery compartment for one AAA battery is on the bottom, marked by two raised ridges; the lid comes off by pressing down and pulling forward. The battery lasts about a year. When it runs low, two short beeps or three very short pulses follow each result.

Features:
Identifies $1, $2, $5, $10, $20, $50 and $100 bills
Voice, tone or vibration modes
Reads bills in any orientation
Flags bills it cannot identify instead of guessing
Two buttons

History:
Orbit Research sells the iBill for $99. The BEP gives one free to any blind or visually impaired US citizen or legal resident through its U.S. Currency Reader Program. Large-print, braille and CD instructions are included. Orbit Research, of Wilmington, Delaware, launched it on December 19, 2009 at $99, about a third of the cost of existing readers, claiming better than 99.9 percent accuracy in under a second. AccessWorld reviewed the first model in May 2010. The second generation, reviewed in December 2012, kept the size but replaced the closed slot with an open guide, recessed the buttons, and added a key ring and a 2.5 mm headphone jack. In vibration mode, $1, $2 and $5 are one, two and three short pulses; $10, $20 and $50 are one, two and three long pulses; $100 is short-long-short-long. Tone mode uses low and high beeps in the same pattern.
