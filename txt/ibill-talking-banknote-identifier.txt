Detailed Description:
The iBill is a small currency reader from Orbit Research. Orbit describes it as an ultra-slim key-fob design, 3.0 × 1.6 × 0.7 inches, that clips to a key ring or lanyard.
To use it, you slide a bill into the slot and press the button on the side. It announces the denomination in a clear female voice, or for privacy as a pattern of tones or vibrations. The vibration mode also serves people who are deaf-blind.
It has just two buttons and a case of engineering-grade polycarbonate. Orbit says most bills are identified in under a second, with better than 99.9% accuracy, in any orientation. Torn or badly defaced bills are reported as unidentifiable rather than guessed.
It runs on a single user-replaceable AAA battery, which Orbit says lasts over a year with typical use. It can be updated to recognize new banknote designs. Orbit sells it with large-print and audio manuals.
Under the BEP's U.S. Currency Reader Program, eligible blind or visually impaired US citizens and legal residents get one iBill free, with lost or broken units replaced through a toll-free line.
