Detailed Description:
The VoiceNote is a BrailleNote without the braille display. Pulse Data and HumanWare introduced it at Closing the Gap 2000, with either an eight-dot braille keyboard (BT) or a QWERTY keyboard (QT). It runs the same Windows CE-based KeySoft suite as the BrailleNote, but output is speech only.
AccessWorld measured it at 9.5 × 5 × 1 inches and 1 pound 10 ounces, the same footprint as the BrailleNote. The case is gray. On the BT model the nine keys (eight braille dots plus a space bar) are green and arranged on a gentle curve for comfort.
Around the case are a PC Card slot, serial and parallel ports, a modem jack, an AC adapter jack, an earphone jack, a CompactFlash slot and an infrared port. Power comes from a rechargeable nickel-metal-hydride battery.
User reports on the BrailleNote list say early BT keys were fairly noisy and clacky and later units were quieter. QT owners described their keyboards as very quiet.
AccessWorld priced it at $2,295. Its strengths were the KeyWord word processor and KeyPlan calendar. You cannot install ordinary Windows programs on it, and AccessWorld found the e-mail and GPS (Atlas Speaks-based) features needed improvement.
