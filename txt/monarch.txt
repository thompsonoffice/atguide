Detailed Description:
The Monarch is a multi-line braille and tactile graphics device from APH and HumanWare. It is about the size of a 15-inch gaming laptop: 15.9 inches wide, 10.5 inches deep and 1.4 inches high, weighing about 4.65 pounds (2.1 kilograms). The NFB calls it a workstation rather than a portable note-taker. It ships in a protective bubble sleeve with a hard carrying case.
Most of the top is the tactile surface: a continuous grid of 96 by 40 evenly spaced pins, 3,840 in all. It shows ten lines of 32 braille cells, or a raised picture across the whole surface. Because the pins are evenly spaced, line spacing can be widened for beginning readers. A user-replaceable membrane protects it from dust and spills.
Nearest you is an eight-key Perkins-style braille keyboard. APH lists up and down arrows, direction pads, Android navigation keys and controls for paging and zooming. Infrared sensors detect touch gestures on the surface, and a vibration motor gives haptic feedback.
For connections, it has a USB-C port, a USB-A port, an HDMI port that shows a sighted teacher or classmate what is on the display, and an audio jack. It also has a microphone, stereo speakers, Wi-Fi and Bluetooth.
When the display refreshes, many pins move at once with an audible clatter, and you must lift your hands for it to refresh correctly. A double press of the refresh button redraws it.

History:
APH partnered with HumanWare just over four years before launch; HumanWare designed the display around the braille cell from the South Korean company Dot, and the NFB took part in development. APH began shipping it to students on September 17, 2024. Apps include KeyWord, a braille editor for BRF and eBRF files, the Tactile Viewer linked to APH's Tactile Graphics Image Library of over two thousand graphics, KeyMath, a graphing calculator built with Desmos, and Victor Reader for Bookshare and NFB-NEWSLINE. It carries a two-year warranty and is available with federal quota funds. The NFB's reviewer noted you must lift your hands as it refreshes, and that refreshing is noisy.
