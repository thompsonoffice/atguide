TripleTalk
Company: Access Solutions
Category: Speech Synthesizers
Released: By April 2001 (TripleTalk PCI reported new); USB and serial models followed

Overview:
A hardware speech synthesizer from Access Solutions, sold in PCI, USB and serial versions and supported by JAWS and, through its serial port, by Speakup on Linux.

Detailed Description:
TripleTalk came in more than one physical form, and each is documented differently. A 2003 paper by Joe Grand (Grand Idea Studio) lists the TripleTalk PCI, a $395 expansion card for desktop PCs, and the TripleTalk USB, a $495 external unit with both serial and USB interfaces, an internal rechargeable battery, an internal speaker, and dimensions of 5.5 x 4.0 x 1.5 inches.
The Linux kernel's Speakup guide groups the external TripleTalk (on a DB-9 serial connection only) with the DoubleTalk LT and LiteTalk under the same ltlk driver, and says Speakup does not support the internal TripleTalk card. No source found gives the layout of controls on the external unit or on the PCI card.
The later TripleTalk USB Mini is the best documented. Blind Mice Mega Mall's listing describes it as palm-sized, 2.4 x 1.6 x 0.8 inches and 1.5 ounces, with a 1.4-inch onboard speaker and a 1/8-inch stereo headphone jack.
The Mini's volume is set with a slider on the side or by software. It is powered entirely by the USB bus, with no battery or power adapter, and its 8 KB flashable EPROM accepts firmware updates in the field.
The Mini's box contains the unit, an installation disk with documentation, a 12-inch full-speed USB cable, 1/8-inch stereo earbuds, and a quick-install reference sheet. Blind Mice lists it at $199.99, with English or Spanish speech and support in JAWS, Window-Eyes and NVDA.

History:
A Linux user reported owning a brand-new TripleTalk PCI from Access Solutions on April 20, 2001; no Linux driver existed then, because the developer declined an open-source driver. By the mid-2000s list members described TripleTalk, with an occasional DECtalk Express, as the only standalone hardware synthesizers still being bought. Its USB model let JAWS speak at a separate volume from the computer's sound card. Some list posts attribute it to RC Systems, the DoubleTalk maker; other posts call it an Access Solutions (Axsol) product.
