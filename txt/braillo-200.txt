Detailed Description:
The Braillo 200 is a heavy production interpoint embosser that sits on a table. The NFB's 1994 evaluation called it half the price and half the speed of the floor-standing Braillo 400S, adding that the table had better be a strong one. Braillo's 1998 manual gives its weight as 145 kg and says it needs about 1.25 by 1.5 metres (4 by 5 feet) of space. The NFB measured it at 34.5 inches wide, 26 inches deep and 23 inches high, and listed its weight as 386 pounds.
Fan-fold paper feeds from a box placed at the back, opposite the operating panel. It passes between two knobs and through a slit in the paper guide, then locks into the tractor feed at the front. A FINE ADJUST button lines the paper up with a notch for the chosen sheet length.
A green switch marked ON and a red switch marked OFF power it, and a green lamp shows when it is on. A small visual display shows messages and three sheet counters: a total that cannot be reset, and Count 1 and Count 2. An audio alarm sounds when there is a problem, but the messages themselves appear only on the display.
Push buttons on the panel include COUNT1/COUNT2, DUMP BUFFER, PRINT VALUES, SCROLL, RESET COUNT, TEST PRINT (which also restarts after paper runs out), RESET PRINTER, I/O, ASCII-TABLES and LAYOUT 1 to 4. Settings are chosen by holding a button and pressing SCROLL until the value you want appears.
It embosses 6- or 8-dot braille, single- or double-sided, at 200 characters per second (about 600 pages an hour), with 10 to 42 characters per line on paper 140 to 330 mm wide and 4 to 14 inches long. It connects by parallel (Centronics) or RS-232 serial, both electrically isolated, and runs on single-phase 220 V. The NFB listed it at $37,995 in 1994.

History:
Braillo, which introduced the world's first double-sided production embosser (the Braillo 270) in January 1980, expanded its line in 1986 with the Braillo 200. Braillo describes it as a compact double-sided embosser known for its reliability and consistent braille quality.
Predecessors: Braillo 270 (Braillo, January 1980)
