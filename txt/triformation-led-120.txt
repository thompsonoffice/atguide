Detailed Description:
The Triformation LED-120 was a full-page braille embosser, the full-sheet successor to the company's BD-3 tape embosser. APH says it became available in June 1974 at $9,000, much more than the BD-3, and was far more popular.
A 1979 University of Iowa braille printer report cited it, alongside SAGEM's TEM/REM 8 BR, as representing the state of the art. It ran at 120 characters per second, against 15 for the SAGEM.
Its electronics were 'random' logic with some LSI chips, and it used a mechanical control system rather than a microprocessor.
It took the ASCII character set and mapped 64 characters directly to the 64 six-dot braille signs. That mapping matched Grade 1 English Braille only for letters, so separate translation software was needed for proper Grade 1 or Grade 2 braille. It also supported an 8-dot cell.
No source found in this research gives the LED-120's physical size, weight or control layout, so those details are not included.

History:
The NFB's Braille Technology Timeline (2026) calls the LED 120 Triformation's popular embosser of the late 1970s, which differs from this entry's earlier 1974 date. It followed the BD 3 (1971), and Triformation later became Enabling Technologies.
Predecessors: Triformation BD-3 (Enabling Technologies (formerly Triformation Systems), 1971)
