Detailed Description:
The Triformation LED-120 was a full-page braille embosser, the full-sheet successor to the company's BD-3 tape embosser. APH says it became available in June 1974 at $9,000, much more than the BD-3, and was far more popular.
In 'A Braille Printer', Andrew W. Appel and Douglas W. Jones of the University of Illinois Medical Computing Laboratory cited it, alongside SAGEM's TEM/REM 8 BR, as representative of the state of the art. They give its speed as 120 characters per second, against 15 for the SAGEM.
The paper says its electronics were 'random' logic with some LSI chips, and that it used a mechanical control system rather than a built-in microcomputer.
It took the ASCII character set and mapped 64 characters directly to the 64 six-dot braille signs, including space. That mapping matched Grade 1 English Braille only for letters, so typical computer output was hard to read and separate translation programs were needed for standard Grade 1 or Grade 2 braille.
No source found in this research gives the LED-120's physical size, weight or control layout. The APH museum record covers only the BD-3, and the Appel and Jones paper discusses only its speed and logic, so those details are not included.

History:
The NFB's Braille Technology Timeline (2026) calls the LED 120 Triformation's popular embosser of the late 1970s, which differs from this entry's earlier 1974 date. It followed the BD 3 (1971), and Triformation later became Enabling Technologies.
Predecessors: Triformation BD-3 (Enabling Technologies (formerly Triformation Systems), 1971)
