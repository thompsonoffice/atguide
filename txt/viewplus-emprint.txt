Detailed Description:
The Emprint, later renamed VP SpotDot, is a desktop braille and tactile-graphics embosser that also prints colour ink, built around an HP inkjet mechanism. ViewPlus calls it the first full-colour ink braille and tactile-graphics printer. It measures 24 inches wide by 7 inches high and 17 inches deep including its extension and input tray (610 by 178 by 432 mm), and weighs 24.4 pounds (11 kg).
Paper sits in an input tray at the front: up to 150 sheets of copy paper, or 60 sheets of braille paper or card stock, or up to 20 envelopes, labels or banners. A fold-out output tray slides onto pins above the input tray. It takes A4, US Letter, Legal and envelopes up to 9 inches wide, on media from copy paper to 110-pound card stock.
The lid on top lifts off without hooks or snaps and must be closed during printing. An HP duplexer, the rear paper-path extension, clips onto the back, released by a button on each side.
It prints ink first from HP 27 (black) and HP 28 (tri-colour) cartridges, waits for a set drying time, then embosses braille and graphics at 20 dots per inch with ViewPlus's patented roller-die mechanism. A calibration aligns the ink with the embossed dots.
It connects to the computer by a single USB port on the back. Power use is 12 watts on standby and up to 110 watts when embossing heavily. It is rated for up to 3,000 pages a month.

History:
On May 16, 2006, ViewPlus Technologies of Corvallis, Oregon, announced Emprint, which it called the world's first braille printer to emboss braille along with matching color ink in a single pass. That let sighted teachers and colleagues read the print above or beside the braille. Raised graphics were printed in color, with darker shades embossed higher. It cost $5,995, including braille translation software, and used ordinary inkjet cartridges and paper.
Predecessors: ViewPlus Tiger Advantage (ViewPlus Technologies, Shipping 2000 (announced 2001))
