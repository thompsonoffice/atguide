Detailed Description:
The Orbit Slate 520 is a five-line, 20-cell braille display from Orbit Research; the Slate 340 has three lines of 40 cells. The Slate 520 is about the length of a letter-size sheet but about 2 inches narrower. It is 1.3 inches thick, like a closed thin laptop, weighs 3.15 pounds, and is made of very smooth plastic.
From the back, the top has a row of cursor-routing buttons, then a braille line, then a separator, then the next braille line, and so on. The separators carry raised vertical lines, with every fifth line more prominent, to help you count across.
At each end of each braille line is a pair of rocker buttons for scrolling. The top line's buttons move one line at a time; the lower ones move further, up to five lines at once.
Near the front is a round direction pad with a Select button in the middle, and a Perkins-style keyboard with oval keys for dots 7 and 8 beside the spacebar, used as Backspace and Enter.
The left edge has a small square power button. The SD card slot is in the middle of an edge toward the right corner. The right edge has the USB-C port for computer connection and charging, and a USB-A port for flash drives. It beeps and vibrates to confirm actions, and came with a padded zip cloth case with a front pocket.

History:
Orbit Research's brochure calls the Slate 520 the world's first multi-line braille display. It suggests uses such as spatial math with fractions and matrices, braille music and spreadsheets. It offers 2D cursor routing, onboard translation in over 40 languages, a book reader, a note-taker and clock, calendar and calculator apps. A Spanish-language review from February 2024 reports that early units shipped late because of component quality problems.
