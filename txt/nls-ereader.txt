Detailed Description:
The NLS Braille eReader from HumanWare is a 20-cell braille reader that the Library of Congress lends free to its braille patrons. The NLS user guide (version 3.0) describes a flat unit with an eight-key Perkins-style keyboard across the back half of the top and the braille display in front of it.
The keyboard is numbered from the centre out: dots 1, 2, 3 and 7 to the left, and 4, 5, 6 and 8 to the right. Dot 7 doubles as Backspace and dot 8 as Enter. In front of the keys are 20 braille cells, each with a cursor-routing button, and two identical Space keys run along the front edge of the top.
On the front edge are five thumb keys, from left to right: Up, Left, a round Select, Right and Down. Each rectangular key has a small tactile ridge showing its direction, and Left and Right have vertical tactile lines on their outer sides.
On the left side, from back to front, are a USB-C port for charging and connecting to a computer or phone, a green power light, an oval Power button with a raised dot, a USB-A port for a flash drive or keyboard, and a lanyard hole. The right side has an audio jack and two volume buttons, which the NLS eReader does not use, plus a second lanyard hole. The back edge has an SD card slot near the left end.
It charges fully in about three hours and runs about 15 hours on a charge. It came with a USB-A to USB-C cable, an AC adapter, an NLS cartridge USB cable, a lanyard, and print and braille getting-started guides.

History:
The National Library Service began piloting free 20-cell braille displays for patrons in 2020, funded by a five-million-dollar Congressional appropriation. Two models were chosen: one by HumanWare, based on the Brailliant BI 20X, and one by Zoomax.
