Detailed Description:
The Franklin Bill Reader, model BR-1000, is a small handheld money identifier. It measures 79 × 42 × 20 mm, about the size of a small pager.
A large CAPTURE button sits in the top corner. You hold the reader 3 to 8 inches above a single U.S. bill, lens facing the bill, and press CAPTURE.
A built-in digital camera takes a picture, and Evolution Robotics' ViPR visual pattern recognition identifies the denomination.
A built-in speaker announces the value in English or Spanish. It recognises current and recent designs of the $1, $2, $5, $10, $20, $50 and $100 bills.
It runs on two AAA batteries, rated for about 1,000 scans or about 200 days on standby.

History:
The New York Times described it in February 2010 as a $300 device using object recognition to name a bill's denomination aloud. It recognized current and recent series, and new designs could be downloaded into it from Franklin's website using a Windows PC. Results came through a built-in speaker or wired earbuds. The article placed it in the context of a 2008 court ruling that the U.S. government discriminated against blind people by not making bills distinguishable.
